# Civic Understanding baseline research pack

**Prepared:** 7 August 2026  
**Purpose:** Editorial research for the five Civic Understanding launch Briefings in the [launch content inventory](../product/launch-content-inventory.md).

> This is research, not public content, an accepted evidence record, or an
> editorial approval. An editor must retrieve and accept the specific source
> used for each published Claim through the Editorial Workflow.

## How to use this pack

The five topic sections provide a durable, neutral mental model and narrowly
worded candidate Claims. Their headings mirror [Briefing Template
v1](../prd/0002-briefing-template-and-reader-experience.md), so an editor can
turn the research into a revision without treating the template as a script.

All direct links were accessed on **7 August 2026**. They are official
Government of Singapore sources (or Singapore Statutes Online). Do not infer a
current number, rate, eligibility result, election outcome, or personal
entitlement from this pack.

### Cross-topic guardrails

- Begin with the durable model. Put changing rules, figures, boundaries,
  service availability, and named office-holders in a dated Current Update.
- Keep a Source distinct from a Claim. A source can support several Claims,
  but each published Claim requires accepted support.
- Explain public institutions and services neutrally. Do not compare parties or
  candidates, advocate a policy, give housing/financial/legal advice, or give
  account-operation/security instructions.
- Capture the source's displayed last-updated date (if present), access date,
  and direct URL when accepting it. Official webpages are living sources.

## 1. How Singapore's Government Works

**Inventory scope:** Three organs of state; how law and policy move; President,
Prime Minister, Cabinet, ministries, and courts.  
**Freshness:** Structural — review at least yearly.

### One-sentence candidate answer

Singapore's constitutional system has three organs of state: the Legislature
makes laws, the Executive directs and carries out government policy, and the
Judiciary independently administers justice.

### 30-second mental model

```text
Constitution
    │
    ├── Legislature: Parliament enacts legislation
    ├── Executive: Cabinet gives the Government its general direction
    └── Judiciary: courts independently administer justice
```

The Constitution is Singapore's supreme law and provides the basic framework
for these organs. The President is Head of State. The Prime Minister is the
effective head of the Executive and chairs Cabinet. This is a map of roles,
not a claim that every public decision follows one identical path.

### Candidate durable Claims

| ID | Candidate Claim | Primary support |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GOV-1 | Singapore's written Constitution is the supreme law and sets the basic framework for the Executive, Legislature, and Judiciary. | [PMO — The Government](https://www.pmo.gov.sg/about-us/the-government/) |
| GOV-2 | Parliament is the legislative authority responsible for enacting legislation. | [PMO — The Government](https://www.pmo.gov.sg/about-us/the-government/) |
| GOV-3 | Cabinet is responsible for the Government's general direction and is accountable to Parliament. | [PMO — The Government](https://www.pmo.gov.sg/about-us/the-government/) |
| GOV-4 | The Prime Minister is the effective head of the Executive and chairs Cabinet. | [PMO — The Government](https://www.pmo.gov.sg/about-us/the-government/) |
| GOV-5 | The Judiciary independently administers justice. | [PMO — The Government](https://www.pmo.gov.sg/about-us/the-government/); [Singapore Courts — legal system](https://www.judiciary.gov.sg/who-we-are/about-legal-system) |
| GOV-6 | The President is Head of State and has specified constitutional powers; outside areas where the Constitution gives discretion, the President acts on Cabinet's advice. | [PMO — The Government](https://www.pmo.gov.sg/about-us/the-government/); [Constitution](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/CONST) |

### Five-minute structure and useful distinctions

1. Start with the three-organs visual before introducing office-holders.
2. Give each organ a card: **job**, **main institutions**, and **a familiar
   example**. A law-making example can run from a Bill in Parliament to an Act;
   a court example should say courts decide cases by applying the law.
3. Add a dedicated **President / Prime Minister / Cabinet** comparison. The
   key distinction is Head of State versus effective head of the Executive;
   Cabinet is the collective central decision-making body of the Executive.
4. Identify a ministry or statutory board only when an accepted source supports
   its role.

### Why people care, questions, and mistakes to avoid

- **Why it matters:** The map lets a reader place Parliament, Cabinet,
  ministries, and courts before discussing a policy or law.
- **Ask:** Which organ has the relevant job? Is the discussion about a proposed
  law, an implemented policy, or a court decision?
- **Avoid:** Placing the President inside Parliament; describing the Prime
  Minister as Head of State; or treating a ministry, statutory board, Cabinet,
  and Parliament as interchangeable.

### Freshness and caveats

This is structural, but constitutional amendments and institutional
arrangements can change. Keep Cabinet membership, current cases, and current
political debate out of the evergreen body; use separately dated Current
Updates.

## 2. Parliament and Elections

**Inventory scope:** Parliament's work, a Bill's path, constituencies, and
voting process.  
**Freshness:** Policy/service — review at least quarterly.

### One-sentence candidate answer

Parliament makes laws, scrutinises the Government and State finances; elections
choose representatives through constituencies, while boundaries, registers, and
election timetables are periodically updated.

### 30-second mental model

```text
Electoral division (constituency)
    → eligible registered elector votes when an election is held
    → MPs represent the constituency in Parliament
    → Parliament debates, scrutinises, and considers Bills
    → a Bill that completes the required process and receives Presidential assent becomes an Act
```

### Candidate durable Claims

| ID | Candidate Claim | Primary support |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PAR-1 | Parliament's functions include making laws, checking Government actions and policies, and scrutinising State finances. | [Parliament — Functions](https://www.parliament.gov.sg/about-us/parliament-information/functions) |
| PAR-2 | A Bill is a draft law; Bills must go through three readings in Parliament and receive the President's assent to become an Act. | [Parliament — Functions](https://www.parliament.gov.sg/about-us/parliament-information/functions); [Passage of a Bill](https://www.parliament.gov.sg/parliamentary-business/glossary/Details/passage-of-a-bill/Passage%20of%20a%20Bill) |
| PAR-3 | Bills are usually introduced by a Minister on behalf of the Government, but any MP may introduce a Private Member's Bill. | [Parliament — Functions](https://www.parliament.gov.sg/about-us/parliament-information/functions) |
| ELE-1 | An electoral division, also called a constituency, is an area demarcated for elections; each has a register of qualified electors. | [ELD — Registers of Electors](https://www.eld.gov.sg/elections.html) |
| ELE-2 | Singapore has Single Member Constituencies (SMCs) and Group Representation Constituencies (GRCs). An SMC returns one MP; a GRC returns a group of MPs. | [ELD — Types of Electoral Divisions](https://www.eld.gov.sg/elections_type_electoral.html) |
| ELE-3 | Electoral boundaries are revised regularly to reflect population growth and shifts. | [ELD — Electoral Divisions](https://www.eld.gov.sg/elections_map_electoral.html) |
| ELE-4 | Parliament normally lasts up to five years from its first sitting unless dissolved sooner, and a general election is held within three months after dissolution. | [Constitution, Articles 65–66](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/CONST) |

### Five-minute structure and useful distinctions

1. Begin with Parliament's three jobs: make laws, scrutinise Government action,
   and scrutinise public finances.
2. Use a Bill-to-law flow: introduction; First Reading; Second Reading and
   debate; detailed consideration; Third Reading; further constitutional steps
   where applicable; then Presidential assent. Do not imply every Bill has
   identical practical timing.
3. Explain **constituency**, **SMC**, **GRC**, **register of electors**, and
   **MP** using ELD terminology. Explain the GRC's minority-representation
   purpose, but not a live list of divisions or candidates.
4. Separate a parliamentary election from a presidential election.

### Why people care, questions, and mistakes to avoid

- **Why it matters:** It shows how a local vote connects to representation and
  how a proposed law differs from a law in force.
- **Ask:** Is this a Bill or an Act? Is a cited constituency map current for
  the relevant election?
- **Avoid:** Saying all Bills are introduced by ministers, presenting a draft
  Bill as law, or reusing old boundaries/registers as current election guidance.

### Freshness and caveats

Election dates, constituency boundaries, number of electors, candidate lists,
party platforms, polling arrangements, and deadlines are time-sensitive.
Verify them against current ELD notices and publish them only as dated Current
Updates.

## 3. HDB and Public Housing

**Inventory scope:** What public housing is; HDB's role; flat types,
home-ownership/resale vocabulary. Grants, prices, and changing policies stay
separate.  
**Freshness:** Policy/service — review at least quarterly.

### One-sentence candidate answer

Public housing in Singapore is planned and delivered through HDB, a statutory
board under the Ministry of National Development, with distinct new-flat,
resale, rental, estate-management, and financing processes.

### 30-second mental model

```text
HDB as statutory board
    ├── plans and develops homes/towns
    ├── administers public-housing sale, rental and related services
    └── supports an ownership/resale system with eligibility and process rules

Buyer/owner journey: plan → check the applicable process → transact → manage the home
```

### Candidate durable Claims

| ID | Candidate Claim | Primary support |
| --- | --- | --- |
| HDB-1 | HDB is a statutory board under the Ministry of National Development. | [HDB financial statements, note 1](https://www.hdb.gov.sg/-/media/hdb-pulse/reports/annual-reports-and-financial-statements/HDB-Financial-Statements-for-the-year-ended-31st-March-2025.pdf) |
| HDB-2 | HDB's principal activities include sale and rental of flats, upgrading and redevelopment of older estates, and mortgage loans for eligible purchasers under public-housing schemes. | [HDB financial statements, note 1](https://www.hdb.gov.sg/-/media/hdb-pulse/reports/annual-reports-and-financial-statements/HDB-Financial-Statements-for-the-year-ended-31st-March-2025.pdf) |
| HDB-3 | HDB describes its role as creating homes and towns for a quality living environment where communities thrive. | [HDB — About us](https://www.hdb.gov.sg/about-us) |
| HDB-4 | HDB distinguishes buying a flat, selling a flat, and managing a home as different service journeys. | [HDB — Buying a Flat](https://www.hdb.gov.sg/buying-a-flat/bto-sbf-and-open-booking-of-flats); [HDB — Home Ownership](https://www.hdb.gov.sg/managing-my-home/home-ownership) |
| HDB-5 | A resale purchase has its own planning, Option to Purchase, application, and completion stages. | [HDB — Process for Buying a Resale Flat](https://www.hdb.gov.sg/buying-a-flat/resale-flats/process-for-buying-a-resale-flat) |

### Five-minute structure and useful distinctions

1. Define public housing as the system delivered and administered through HDB,
   not as a promise that every household qualifies for the same option.
2. Introduce HDB's homes/towns, sales/rental, estate renewal, and service roles
   before buying vocabulary.
3. Compare the **new-flat route** and **resale route**. They are different
   pathways with their own application/transaction steps—not eligibility
   calculators.
4. Define only local terms needed to read the process, such as *resale flat*,
   *Option to Purchase*, *HDB Flat Eligibility letter*, and *Minimum Occupation
   Period*. Verify the current wording/rules when publishing.

### Why people care, questions, and mistakes to avoid

- **Why it matters:** Housing is both a home and a public system involving
  planning, supply, transactions, financing, and neighbourhood life.
- **Ask:** Is this about a new flat, a resale flat, or managing an existing
  home? Is it a durable process description or a current rule?
- **Avoid:** Treating HDB as only a flat seller, treating resale prices as a
  policy rule, or giving personalised financial/eligibility advice.

### Freshness and caveats

Eligibility, grants, income ceilings, loan terms, allocation exercises,
occupation-period rules, quotas, supply, and prices change. Resale transaction
data and availability are especially time-sensitive. Keep numbers/current
availability out of the evergreen baseline unless dated and reviewed.

## 4. CPF

**Inventory scope:** Compulsory savings framework, account purposes,
contribution basics, and links to housing, healthcare, and retirement. Not
personal financial advice.  
**Freshness:** Policy/service — review at least quarterly.

### One-sentence candidate answer

CPF is Singapore's compulsory social-security savings system: eligible working
Singaporeans and permanent residents generally receive employer and employee
contributions, allocated to accounts for purposes including housing, healthcare,
and retirement.

### 30-second mental model

```text
Work and CPF contributions
    → CPF accounts hold savings for different purposes
        ├── Ordinary Account: housing and other permitted uses
        ├── MediSave Account: healthcare and approved insurance uses
        └── Special / later Retirement Account: retirement-related savings
    → CPF LIFE provides retirement-income payouts under its scheme
```

### Candidate durable Claims

| ID | Candidate Claim | Primary support |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CPF-1 | CPF is a social-security system that helps working Singapore citizens and permanent residents set aside funds for retirement and also addresses healthcare, home ownership, family protection, and asset enhancement. | [CPF Board — What is CPF?](https://www.cpf.gov.sg/service/article/what-is-central-provident-fund-cpf) |
| CPF-2 | Working Singaporeans and their employers make monthly CPF contributions, subject to the applicable rules. | [CPF Board — What is CPF?](https://www.cpf.gov.sg/service/article/what-is-central-provident-fund-cpf); [CPF overview](https://www.cpf.gov.sg/member/cpf-overview) |
| CPF-3 | CPF savings are organised into accounts with different purposes, including the Ordinary Account for permitted housing uses and MediSave for healthcare and approved medical-insurance uses. | [CPF Board — What is CPF?](https://www.cpf.gov.sg/service/article/what-is-central-provident-fund-cpf) |
| CPF-4 | CPF's overview says a Retirement Account is created at age 55 and the Special Account is closed at that point. | [CPF overview](https://www.cpf.gov.sg/member/cpf-overview) |
| CPF-5 | CPF LIFE is CPF Board's national longevity-insurance annuity scheme, providing monthly payouts for as long as a member lives, subject to the scheme's rules. | [CPF overview](https://www.cpf.gov.sg/member/cpf-overview); [CPF retirement income](https://www.cpf.gov.sg/member/plan-your-finances/retirement-income) |

### Five-minute structure and useful distinctions

1. Lead with the framework: CPF is not one ordinary bank account, nor only a
   retirement scheme. It connects permitted uses across housing, healthcare,
   and retirement.
2. Use labelled account cards with a plain-language purpose. Do not turn them
   into advice to withdraw, invest, or make a housing decision.
3. Use a life-stage flow: contributions while working → accounts with different
   purposes → retirement arrangements. Explain CPF LIFE separately from the
   accounts.
4. Use **contribution rate**, **CPF savings**, **account**, **Retirement
   Account**, and **CPF LIFE** as article-local terms. Link to CPF Board's
   current tools rather than interpreting a reader's balance/entitlement.

### Why people care, questions, and mistakes to avoid

- **Why it matters:** CPF affects conversations about pay, a home, healthcare,
  and retirement, but the relevant rules depend on context.
- **Ask:** Which account/purpose is involved? Is this a framework claim or a
  current rate/limit? Does it need qualified personalised advice?
- **Avoid:** Quoting a rate without date, age/wage context, and conditions;
  calling CPF savings freely interchangeable cash; or presenting this as
  financial advice.

### Freshness and caveats

Contribution/allocation rates, limits, interest, withdrawal conditions, CPF
LIFE choices/payouts, and account rules can change. The baseline should explain
purposes and relationships; figures need an effective date and quarterly check.

## 5. Digital Government

**Inventory scope:** Digital public services, national digital identity,
service design, and digital inclusion. Avoid sensitive account-operation
advice.  
**Freshness:** Policy/service — review at least quarterly.

### One-sentence candidate answer

Digital Government is Singapore's effort to make public transactions more
accessible, seamless, and secure, using shared digital services such as
Singpass while pairing digital delivery with inclusion support.

### 30-second mental model

```text
Person or business needs a public service
    → proves identity / authorisation where required
    → uses a government digital service
    → service is designed to reduce repeated steps and protect the transaction

Digital access and skills support are part of the model, not an afterthought.
```

### Candidate durable Claims

| ID | Candidate Claim | Primary support |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DIG-1 | GovTech describes Singapore's digital-government aim as making government transactions easy, seamless, secure, accessible, and better for citizens and businesses. | [GovTech — Digital services](https://www.tech.gov.sg/products-and-services/for-citizens/digital-services/) |
| DIG-2 | Singpass is a trusted digital identity that enables residents to access government and business services online. | [GovTech — Singpass](https://www.tech.gov.sg/products-and-services/for-citizens/digital-services/singpass/) |
| DIG-3 | Singapore's National Digital Identity includes a citizen-facing identity layer such as Singpass and a corporate authorisation system, Corppass. | [GovTech — Digital identity](https://www.tech.gov.sg/about-us/what-we-do/our-digital-government-efforts/digital-identity/) |
| DIG-4 | GovTech identifies authentication, pre-filled information for participating services, and digital signing as parts of the Singpass experience. | [GovTech — Singpass](https://www.tech.gov.sg/products-and-services/for-citizens/digital-services/singpass/) |
| DIG-5 | Digital inclusion includes access to devices/connectivity and digital skills; IMDA's Digital Access Programme supports groups including low-income households, students, and persons with disabilities. | [IMDA — Digital Access](https://www.imda.gov.sg/how-we-can-help/digital-access); [IMDA — Singapore Digital Society Report](https://www.imda.gov.sg/about-imda/research-and-statistics/singapore-digital-society-report) |

### Five-minute structure and useful distinctions

1. Start with the outcome: a public service should be easier to use without
   making security or inclusion disappear.
2. Separate **digital service** (the transaction), **digital identity** (how a
   person may prove identity), and **authorisation** (what someone can do for
   an organisation).
3. Use a system-level example: a person authenticates to access a service;
   participating services may use consented data to reduce repeated form
   filling. Do not give account instructions or say every service works alike.
4. Give digital inclusion equal visual weight: access, connectivity, skills,
   and assisted routes matter when services are digital.

### Why people care, questions, and mistakes to avoid

- **Why it matters:** Digital public services affect common tasks, but must
  remain understandable, inclusive, and secure.
- **Ask:** Is the issue identity, authorisation, service design, data use,
  access, or skills? Does the service have an official assisted route?
- **Avoid:** Equating Singpass with every government service, assuming equal
  access/eligibility, publishing account-recovery steps, or repeating
  potentially stale security advice.

### Freshness and caveats

Service counts, authentication features, eligibility, app screens, support
schemes, and security guidance change frequently. Link to the current official
GovTech, Singpass, or IMDA page rather than reproducing sensitive operational
steps.

## Editorial hand-off checklist

1. Retrieve each direct source again and record its access date, displayed
   date, provenance, and rights status.
2. Split candidate Claims into atomic statements; accept support for each.
3. Check every Template v1 section: mental model, comparison/flow when useful,
   local key terms, entities, debates, questions, mistakes, Sources, and
   freshness.
4. Remove dated figures, names, eligibility results, and live developments
   unless they become a separately labelled/reviewed Current Update.
5. Have the human editor check accuracy, plain-English clarity, neutrality, and
   whether final wording overstates the source.
