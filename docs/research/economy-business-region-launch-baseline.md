# Economy, business, and region: baseline research pack

**Prepared:** 7 August 2026  
**For:** the six controlled-launch Topics in the Economy, business, and region
section of the [launch content inventory](../product/launch-content-inventory.md).  
**Purpose:** research leads for human editorial review—not publishable copy,
accepted Sources, or investment, trade, or foreign-policy advice.

This pack translates primary and official material into deliberately narrow
candidate Claims and a reader-first Briefing Template v1 outline. It is
designed to help an editor start a Source Submission and decide what to verify,
not to bypass the [Editorial Workflow](../prd/0003-editorial-workflow.md).

## How to use this pack

1. Retrieve the linked source itself when creating a Source Submission. Check
   its publication date, the exact passage, rights, and whether it remains the
   right authority.
2. Treat each item under **Candidate Claims** as a separate Claim to verify and
   support. The links are direct, primary or official leads; they are not
   pre-accepted Sources.
3. Preserve the boundary between a durable explanation and a **Current Update**.
   Numbers, rankings, programme terms, trade measures, diplomatic events and
   predictions are normally Current Update material unless an editor explicitly
   rechecks them.
4. Keep contested assessments, live conflicts, forecasts, country comparisons,
   political attribution and investment recommendations out of these baseline
   Briefings.

## Shared editorial guardrails

- Use *Singapore’s Economy* to explain a model, not to say whether the economy
  is doing “well” now.
- Use *Trade and the Port* and *Global Supply Chains* to distinguish physical
  movement, border procedures and value creation. A container passing through a
  port is not necessarily a Singapore export.
- Use *Startups and Venture Capital* to explain roles and funding vocabulary;
  do not recommend companies, funds, investments or eligibility outcomes.
- Use *ASEAN* to explain an organisation and its decision-making convention;
  do not imply it is a supranational government or that consensus guarantees a
  single public position on every issue.
- Use *Regional Geopolitics as Durable Background* to give readers a map of
  concepts and institutions. It must not analyse an ongoing dispute, predict
  outcomes, rank countries, or attribute motives.

## 1. Singapore's Economy

### Reader mental model

Singapore's economy can be introduced as a **connected system**: people and
firms create goods and services; firms sell to local and overseas customers;
and public institutions provide rules, infrastructure, skills and monetary
stability. GDP is one measurement of production within Singapore, not a score
for every aspect of life.

```text
People, firms and capabilities
              ↓
 Goods-producing + services industries
              ↓
 Local demand + trade with the world
              ↓
     Output, jobs and incomes
```

### Candidate Claims

1. **GDP measures production within Singapore's economic territory.** The
   Department of Statistics defines GDP as the aggregate value of goods and
   services produced within Singapore's economic territory and says it compiles
   estimates by output, expenditure and income approaches. [SingStat,
   *Singapore Economy*](https://www.singstat.gov.sg/modules/infographics/economy)
2. **Services and goods-producing industries are both material parts of the
   economy.** SingStat's 2025 infographic reports that services generated more
   than 70% of nominal value added and goods-producing industries about 25%.
   This is a dated statistic, not an evergreen proportion. [SingStat,
   *Singapore Economy*](https://www.singstat.gov.sg/modules/infographics/economy)
3. **Industry composition is more useful than a single GDP number for a first
   map.** SingStat presents industry-level output, including wholesale trade,
   manufacturing, finance and insurance, transport and storage, and information
   and communications. The editor should use the current table/infographic if
   naming sectors. [SingStat, 2025 economy infographic
   PDF](https://www.singstat.gov.sg/modules/infographics/-/media/Files/visualising_data/infographics/Economy/singapore-economy03032026.pdf)
4. **Productivity is about output in relation to inputs, not simply working
   harder.** The Singapore economy infographic presents change in value added
   per worker as a productivity measure; define the measure cautiously and link
   to its statistical metadata before publication. [SingStat,
   *Singapore Economy*](https://www.singstat.gov.sg/modules/infographics/economy)
5. **Economic measures have limits.** GDP is an output measure; it is not on
   its own a measure of household distribution, unpaid work, environmental
   impact, or wellbeing. This is a definitional boundary inferred from the
   official GDP definition, so present it as a scope explanation, not a claim
   that GDP is unimportant. [SingStat,
   *Singapore Economy*](https://www.singstat.gov.sg/modules/infographics/economy)

### Freshness and verification notes

- **Category:** Data/industry; review at least quarterly.
- All GDP levels, growth rates, sector shares, employment, productivity figures,
  trade values, forecasts and rankings require a release date and should be
  refreshed from SingStat or MTI before publication.
- Do not use a quarterly economic survey to make a permanent claim about which
  sector “drives” the economy. It is a dated snapshot.
- Confirm each statistical definition and reference period in the published
  table, not just on an infographic.

### Briefing Template v1 outline

- **One-sentence explanation:** Singapore's economy is the network of people,
  businesses and institutions that produces goods and services here and trades
  with the world.
- **30-second overview:** Explain GDP; show services and goods-producing
  industries; say that numbers change and will be dated.
- **Five-minute explanation:** Follow the mental-model flow from capabilities
  through industries and trade to output/jobs/incomes; define value added and
  productivity in plain English.
- **Why people care:** jobs, wages, prices, business opportunities and public
  resources are all discussed using economic language, though no single number
  settles those questions.
- **Key terms:** GDP, value added, nominal/real (only if carefully sourced),
  productivity, industry, exports, imports.
- **Entities and Singapore/SEA angle:** SingStat, MTI, MAS; Singapore's regional
  and global connections without current performance commentary.
- **Debates:** Which measures best describe progress; productivity and costs;
  openness and resilience. Frame as questions, not conclusions.
- **Questions / mistakes to avoid:** “What does this number measure and over
  what period?”; do not confuse GDP growth with every household's experience.
- **Sources and review date:** current SingStat data plus a dated MTI survey;
  visibly state the data vintage.

## 2. Trade and the Port

### Reader mental model

Singapore's port is part of a wider trade system. **Ships and terminals move
cargo; shipping and logistics firms organise it; Customs applies rules and
permits; and other maritime services support the movement.** Transhipment means
cargo is transferred between vessels on its journey, so it need not enter
Singapore's domestic market.

```text
Seller → shipping line → Singapore port → another vessel → buyer
                   ↘ Customs, terminals, logistics and marine services ↗
```

### Candidate Claims

1. **Singapore's port connects shipping lines and ports around the world.** MPA
   describes connectivity as a hallmark of the port and identifies shipping
   lines, global port links and transhipment as part of its hub role. Its
   numerical figures should be refreshed rather than copied as evergreen copy.
   [MPA, *Global Connectivity*](https://www.mpa.gov.sg/maritime-singapore/what-maritime-singapore-offers/global-hub-port)
2. **A port is more than a place where containers are loaded.** MPA's hub-page
   navigation identifies services such as bunkering, ship registry, marine
   insurance, shipping finance, legal and arbitration services, and shipbroking.
   Explain these as examples of a maritime ecosystem, not a complete list.
   [MPA, *Global Connectivity*](https://www.mpa.gov.sg/maritime-singapore/what-maritime-singapore-offers/global-hub-port)
3. **Customs both collects relevant revenue and enables legitimate trade.**
   Singapore Customs describes its dual role as revenue protection and trade
   facilitation, including administration of import, export and transhipment
   rules. [Singapore Customs, *Organisation overview*](https://www.customs.gov.sg/about-us/organisation-overview/rank-structure/)
4. **International movement of goods has regulatory steps as well as physical
   steps.** Singapore Customs says its business guidance covers import, export,
   transhipment, permits, documentation, classification, valuation and rules of
   origin. [Singapore Customs, *Doing Business*](https://www.customs.gov.sg/doing-business/)
5. **Port throughput is a dated measure, not a permanent descriptor.** MPA
   reported 44.66 million TEUs of container throughput in 2025; include it only
   with the year and source, or keep it in a Current Update. [MPA, 13 January
   2026 release](https://www.mpa.gov.sg/media-centre/details/singapore-posts-record-port-performance-in-2025-and-develops-future-readiness-through-industry-collaborations-for-2026)

### Freshness and verification notes

- **Category:** Data/industry; review quarterly.
- Route counts, vessel calls, throughput, shipping rankings, terminal plans,
  fuel-sales figures and awards are volatile or dated.
- Legal and compliance details can change; use Customs' current operational
  guidance but do not turn the Briefing into regulatory advice.
- Avoid asserting that every cargo movement creates the same local economic
  value; use an editor-reviewed explanation of transhipment and services.

### Briefing Template v1 outline

- **One sentence / 30 seconds:** The port connects cargo, ships, services and
  border processes; it is a hub, not merely a warehouse.
- **Five minutes:** trace one fictional container from arrival, transfer and
  documentation to departure; distinguish port, terminal, transhipment,
  shipping line, freight forwarder and Customs.
- **Why people care:** trade supports access to products and business activity,
  while ports also need safety, reliability, land and environmental planning.
- **Visual:** accessible flow above, with a separate support-services card.
- **Debates:** reliability, security, digitalisation and decarbonisation—keep
  current policy choices in updates.
- **Questions / mistakes:** “Did the cargo enter Singapore, or transfer here?”;
  do not equate container counts with the value of domestic exports.

## 3. Startups and Venture Capital

### Reader mental model

A startup is an early-stage business trying to develop and grow a repeatable
product or service. Its ecosystem can include founders, employees, customers,
research partners, incubators/accelerators, investors and public-support
programmes. **Venture capital (VC) is one form of equity investment, not a
default pathway or a verdict on whether a business is good.**

```text
Problem + founders → product and customers → evidence of traction
      ↘ mentors, programmes, partners, employees, different funding options ↗
```

### Candidate Claims

1. **Startup ecosystems include more than startups.** Enterprise Singapore
   describes Startup SG as coordinating initiatives for founders, talent,
   funding and networks, and lists support for incubators, accelerators,
   investors and infrastructure. [Enterprise Singapore, *Join Startup SG*](https://www.enterprisesg.gov.sg/grow-your-business/partner-with-singapore/innovation-and-startups/join-startup-sg)
2. **Funding support has different purposes and stages.** The same official
   page distinguishes founder mentorship/funding, early-stage technology
   commercialisation, government-backed loans, and investor co-investment.
   Explain the categories without implying a particular business qualifies.
   [Enterprise Singapore, *Join Startup SG*](https://www.enterprisesg.gov.sg/grow-your-business/partner-with-singapore/innovation-and-startups/join-startup-sg)
3. **Startup SG Equity uses several investment modalities.** Enterprise
   Singapore's 2026 factsheet describes co-investment alongside independent
   investors, direct investments in selected growth-stage deep-tech startups,
   and a fund-of-funds modality investing in VC firms that then invest in
   eligible startups. These are programme details and must be treated as dated.
   [Enterprise Singapore, *Startup SG Equity factsheet*](https://www.enterprisesg.gov.sg/-/media/esg/files/media-centre/media-releases/2026/march/mr01026_startup-sg-equity.pdf)
4. **A VC fund and a startup have different roles.** The official co-investment
   description distinguishes fund managers/VC firms from the startups they may
   back. This supports a simple explanation: a VC firm manages investment
   capital; a startup operates the business. [Enterprise Singapore,
   *SEEDS Capital*](https://www.enterprisesg.gov.sg/financial-support/seeds-capital)
5. **Public support does not remove private decision-making or business risk.**
   Enterprise Singapore describes co-investment with qualified third-party
   investors and assessments based on defined criteria. Avoid treating public
   programmes as endorsements or guarantees. [Enterprise Singapore, *Startup SG
   Equity*](https://www.enterprisesg.gov.sg/resources/media-centre/media-releases/2026/march/mr01026-startup-sg-equity)

### Freshness and verification notes

- **Category:** Data/industry; review quarterly.
- Funding totals, deal counts, valuation labels, scheme caps, tax incentives,
  eligibility, partners and named portfolio companies are all dated or
  programme-specific. Verify them immediately before publication.
- Do not use promotional language such as “leading hub” as a factual baseline
  Claim without defining the measure and adding independent official evidence.
- Do not give advice on raising capital, choosing a fund or investing.

### Briefing Template v1 outline

- **One sentence / 30 seconds:** A startup is a young business; VC is one type
  of investment that may help some startups grow in exchange for ownership.
- **Five minutes:** follow a fictional product from idea and customer testing
  to possible funding; distinguish grant, loan, equity investment, incubator,
  accelerator, angel investor, VC fund, co-investment and fund of funds.
- **Why people care:** these terms appear in jobs, technology, business and
  public-investment conversations, but funding is neither the only measure of
  usefulness nor a consumer recommendation.
- **Singapore/SEA angle:** use Startup SG and local ecosystem institutions as
  examples, subject to current programme verification.
- **Debates:** risk, patient capital, public support and commercial outcomes;
  identify them as trade-offs rather than promises.
- **Mistakes:** do not call a grant an investment; do not assume VC funding is
  revenue, profit, or guaranteed success.

## 4. ASEAN

### Reader mental model

ASEAN is a regional association in which member states cooperate. It is **not a
single government above its members**. Its Charter sets shared purposes,
principles, institutions and a consultation-and-consensus approach; each member
state remains responsible for its own domestic government and laws.

```text
ASEAN member states
        ↓ cooperate through ASEAN institutions
Political-security | Economic | Socio-cultural community work
        ↓ consultation and consensus for ASEAN decisions
```

### Candidate Claims

1. **ASEAN is governed by its Charter.** The Charter sets out ASEAN's purposes,
   principles, legal personality, organs and decision-making provisions.
   [ASEAN, *ASEAN Charter*](https://asean.org/wp-content/uploads/images/archive/publications/ASEAN-Charter.pdf)
2. **Consultation and consensus are the basic decision-making principle in the
   Charter.** Article 20 says this explicitly, while also providing that the
   ASEAN Summit may decide how a specific decision is made where consensus is
   not achieved. [ASEAN Charter, Article 20](https://asean.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/2.-February-2015-The-ASEAN-Charter-18th-Reprint.pdf)
3. **ASEAN's stated principles include respect for sovereignty, territorial
   integrity, non-interference and peaceful settlement of disputes.** Attribute
   this to the Charter and do not infer how any current dispute will be resolved.
   [ASEAN, *ASEAN Charter*](https://asean.org/wp-content/uploads/images/archive/publications/ASEAN-Charter.pdf)
4. **The ASEAN Community has political-security, economic and socio-cultural
   pillars.** Singapore MFA describes the three Community pillars and Singapore
   as one of ASEAN's founding members. The current membership and any live
   institutional development should be checked separately. [MFA, *Association
   of Southeast Asian Nations*](https://www.mfa.gov.sg/about-mfa/foreign-policy/international-organisations/association-of-southeast-asian-nations-asean/)
5. **ASEAN's consensus model is a process, not proof that all members have the
   same interests or domestic policies.** This is a careful inference from the
   Charter's state-based structure and Article 20. Use it to avoid a common
   reader mistake, and label it as an explanation rather than a quoted rule.
   [ASEAN Charter, Article 20](https://asean.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/2.-February-2015-The-ASEAN-Charter-18th-Reprint.pdf)

### Freshness and verification notes

- **Category:** Policy/service; review quarterly.
- The Charter is durable; leadership, meetings, joint statements, membership
  developments, trade agreements, implementation and diplomatic events are not.
- Never cite an old MFA page's “recent highlights” as current regional context.
- Do not characterise an ASEAN statement as binding law without legal review.

### Briefing Template v1 outline

- **One sentence / 30 seconds:** ASEAN is a regional association through which
  Southeast Asian states cooperate; it does not replace national governments.
- **Five minutes:** explain the Charter, member-state role, community pillars,
  key organs (only from the Charter), consultation and consensus.
- **Why people care:** ASEAN appears in discussion of travel, business,
  regional cooperation and security; readers need to know what it can and
  cannot do.
- **Visual:** member states → ASEAN institutions → three community pillars,
  with consent/consultation displayed as a decision process.
- **Questions / mistakes:** “Is this an ASEAN decision, a national decision or
  a statement?”; do not describe ASEAN as a federal government.
- **Sources/freshness:** Charter plus a current official ASEAN/MFA institutional
  page; dated developments belong in Current Updates.

## 5. Global Supply Chains

### Reader mental model

A supply chain is the linked path through which materials, components,
information, finance and finished goods move between organisations. A global
value chain focuses on how work and value are distributed across those steps.
The chain is not one straight line: a disruption, rule or delay at one point
can affect timing and costs elsewhere.

```text
Design → materials/components → manufacture → transport/border clearance
      → distribution → customer
             ↑ information, compliance, payments and inventory run alongside
```

### Candidate Claims

1. **Companies can divide design, component production, assembly and marketing
   across economies.** WTO describes these as international production chains,
   often called global value chains. [WTO, *Global Value Chains*](https://www.wto.org/english/res_e/statis_e/miwi_e/miwi_e.htm)
2. **A product's final export location does not always show where all its value
   was created.** WTO warns that assigning full commercial value to the last
   country of origin can distort analysis, and provides trade-in-value-added
   data alongside conventional trade data. [WTO, *Global Value Chains*](https://www.wto.org/english/res_e/statis_e/miwi_e/miwi_e.htm)
3. **Trade facilitation concerns the processes for moving goods across borders.**
   The WTO describes it as simplifying, modernising and harmonising import,
   export and transit processes. [WTO, *Trade facilitation*](https://www.wto.org/english/tratop_e/tradfa_e/tradfa_e.htm)
4. **The WTO Trade Facilitation Agreement includes rules on publication,
   pre-arrival processing, release and clearance, and cooperation between border
   agencies.** Explain these as examples of cross-border process design; do not
   claim any specific country implements every provision in the same way.
   [WTO, *Agreement on Trade Facilitation*](https://www.wto.org/english/docs_e/legal_e/tfa_e.htm)
5. **Singapore's Customs arrangements illustrate the connection between trade
   movement and compliance.** Customs says its framework covers import, export,
   transhipment, permits and regulatory controls while facilitating legitimate
   trade. [Singapore Customs, *Legal framework and compliance*](https://www.customs.gov.sg/doing-business/compliance/legal-framework-and-compliance-overview/)

### Freshness and verification notes

- **Category:** Data/industry; review quarterly.
- The basic model is durable. Freight rates, shortages, shipping delays,
  sanctions, tariffs, company sourcing, trade policy changes and disruption
  narratives are Current Update material.
- Be exact about whether a number measures gross trade value, value added,
  intermediate goods, containers, shipments or prices.
- Avoid saying a particular event “caused” a price change without rigorous,
  appropriate evidence; a Briefing can explain possible pathways instead.

### Briefing Template v1 outline

- **One sentence / 30 seconds:** Global supply chains divide the making and
  moving of a product across many businesses and places.
- **Five minutes:** follow a neutral everyday product through the flow above;
  add inventory, shipping, customs clearance and distribution without naming
  an active crisis.
- **Why people care:** supply-chain terms help people understand why products
  and business operations depend on more than a shop or factory.
- **Key terms:** supply chain, value chain, intermediate good, transhipment,
  inventory, lead time, tariff, customs clearance, trade facilitation.
- **Singapore/SEA angle:** Singapore as a logistics and trade node, using the
  separate Trade and the Port Briefing for detail.
- **Mistakes:** a supply chain is not just a shipping route; a good's final
  origin does not by itself show every contribution to its value.

## 6. Regional Geopolitics as Durable Background

### Reader mental model

This Briefing is a **map, not a live scoreboard**. Regional geopolitics means
how geography, sovereign states, trade routes, international rules and
institutions shape the choices governments make. The baseline should help a
reader locate an issue, recognise the relevant forums and separate durable
principles from live events.

```text
Geography and sea/air connections
              +
Sovereign states and national interests
              +
Rules and forums (UN, ASEAN, other agreements)
              ↓
 Cooperation, negotiation, competition and dispute-management
```

### Candidate Claims

1. **The UN Charter provides a durable reference point for key international
   principles.** Article 2 includes sovereign equality, peaceful settlement of
   disputes, and refraining from the threat or use of force against a state's
   territorial integrity or political independence. [United Nations, *Charter
   full text*](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/un-charter/full-text)
2. **Regional arrangements can sit alongside the UN framework.** Article 52
   recognises regional arrangements and agencies for matters appropriate for
   regional action, provided they are consistent with the Charter's purposes
   and principles. [United Nations, *Charter full text*](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/un-charter/full-text)
3. **Singapore MFA describes core foreign-policy objectives in terms of
   sovereignty, a peaceful regional environment, international relevance and
   political/economic space.** Attribute these to MFA's institutional mission;
   do not turn them into a claim about another state's intentions. [MFA,
   *Vision, mission and values*](https://www.mfa.gov.sg/about-mfa/vision-mission-and-values/)
4. **Singapore's official foreign-policy discussion identifies continued access
   to essential supplies and open air and sea lines of communication as core
   considerations.** This is a durable explanation of why connectivity matters,
   not a prediction about a particular route or conflict. [MFA, 2024 SMUN
   remarks](https://www.mfa.gov.sg/newsroom/press-statements-transcripts-and-photos/2min-smun-speech-240604-05-jun-2024/)
5. **ASEAN is one regional forum, while the UN is a global organisation.**
   Explain their different scope and institutional role without implying one
   controls the other. [ASEAN Charter](https://asean.org/wp-content/uploads/images/archive/publications/ASEAN-Charter.pdf); [UN Charter](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/un-charter/full-text)

### Freshness and verification notes

- **Category:** Policy/service; review quarterly, and run a special editorial
  risk check before any revision that touches an ongoing conflict, territorial
  question, sanctions, election or official diplomatic position.
- Use only durable geography, institutional design, treaty text and clearly
  dated official policy statements in the baseline.
- Put summits, statements, military incidents, negotiations, forecasts,
  alliances, leadership comments and country-specific disputes in a Current
  Update—if they are in scope at all—and obtain stronger multi-source review.
- Avoid maps that imply a position on borders, sovereignty or territorial
  claims. If a map is ever needed, use an editorially reviewed, neutral source
  and accessible text alternatives.

### Briefing Template v1 outline

- **One sentence / 30 seconds:** Regional geopolitics is the context created by
  geography, sovereign states and shared rules—not a prediction of what happens
  next.
- **Five minutes:** define sovereignty, national interest, rules-based order,
  multilateralism, regional forum, sea lines of communication and diplomacy;
  use the four-part mental model above.
- **Why people care:** it gives readers a way to interpret references to ASEAN,
  the UN, trade routes and official statements without mistaking them for a
  live-news explainer.
- **Singapore/SEA angle:** explain the region's connectivity and Singapore's
  stated foreign-policy objectives, accurately attributed to MFA.
- **Debates:** frame universally as questions—how should states cooperate and
  manage differences? Do not adjudicate active claims or positions.
- **Questions / mistakes:** “Is this a treaty rule, an official statement, or a
  commentator's assessment?”; do not assume a regional body can decide a
  member state's domestic policy.

## Editorial hand-off checklist

Before creating any draft, the editor should be able to answer yes to all of
these:

- [ ] Each candidate Claim is separated, dated where necessary, and has a
  retrieved primary/official Source.
- [ ] The opening gives the reader a reusable mental model before terminology.
- [ ] Statistics, rankings, programme rules and live developments are either
  current and dated or excluded from the baseline.
- [ ] The Briefing does not make investment recommendations, legal/compliance
  advice, geopolitical predictions, or judgements about active disputes.
- [ ] Sources, Claim support and a review date will be visible to readers after
  Editorial Approval.
