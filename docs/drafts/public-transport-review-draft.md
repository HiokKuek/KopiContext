## One-sentence explanation

Public transport is the shared system of rail, buses, walking/cycling links and interchanges that helps people make everyday journeys across Singapore. [[S1]](#s1) [[S4]](#s4)

## 30-second overview

A public-transport journey is usually more than the ride itself. A person gets to a stop or station, uses a bus or rail service, may transfer at an interchange, and completes the last part of the trip by walking, wheeling, cycling or another mode. LTA frames walking, cycling and public transport as complementary parts of a car-lite transport system. [[S4]](#s4)

Rail provides a network backbone, while buses connect different places and can bring people to rail stations and interchanges. LTA plans and develops the system; rail and bus operators provide services within frameworks set by LTA. [[S1]](#s1) [[S2]](#s2) [[S3]](#s3)

## The five-minute explanation

### A journey is a chain of connections

A useful way to read a transport issue is to ask which part of the trip is affected. Someone may have a short rail ride but a long walk to the station. Another person may need a feeder bus before the train. A third may be concerned about changing services at an interchange.

That is why a public-transport system needs more than vehicles. It also needs access, stations and stops, connections, operating services, fares and planning. LTA's public-transport work covers rail and bus networks, while its broader “getting around” framing includes walking and cycling. [[S1]](#s1) [[S4]](#s4)

### LTA, operators and PTC have different jobs

LTA's role is to plan, develop, regulate and maintain land-transport infrastructure and systems. For rail, its official description distinguishes LTA's builder/regulator role from operators that provide rail services. [[S1]](#s1) [[S2]](#s2)

For buses, LTA's Bus Contracting Model has LTA licensing operators to run public bus services. [[S3]](#s3) This is different from the Public Transport Council's role in regulating public-transport fares and reviewing fare adjustments. [[S5]](#s5)

The distinction matters because a conversation about a delayed bus, a new rail line and a fare adjustment may involve different parts of the system. They should not be treated as the same kind of decision.

### What “trunk”, “feeder” and “interchange” mean

A **trunk service** is a bus service that connects neighbourhoods and towns. A **feeder service** connects MRT stations and bus interchanges with nearby estates or areas. [[S3]](#s3)

An **interchange** is a place where a person can change between services or modes. It is a practical part of a network: it helps turn separate bus and rail services into a connected journey. This is a plain-language explanation of the role shown in LTA's network material, not a claim that all interchanges have the same layout or connections. [[S1]](#s1) [[S3]](#s3)

### A practical example: home to a workplace

Imagine a person travelling from home to work.

1. They walk, wheel or cycle to a nearby bus stop. This is the **access** part of the trip. [[S4]](#s4)
2. They take a feeder bus to an MRT station. Feeder services are designed to connect nearby areas with MRT stations or bus interchanges. [[S3]](#s3)
3. They use rail for the main part of the journey. Rail is one part of the broader public-transport system. [[S1]](#s1) [[S2]](#s2)
4. They leave the station and walk to the workplace. That final leg is still part of whether the whole trip feels usable. [[S4]](#s4)

This is an orientation example, not a claim about a particular route, fare, travel time, accessibility feature or service level.

## Why people care

Public transport shapes how people reach work, school, care, family, recreation and everyday services. It also affects how Singapore uses limited land and road space.

The system raises recurring questions about coverage, reliability, affordability, safety and accessibility. A useful first question is not “is public transport good or bad?” but **which part of the journey or network is being discussed?**

## Relevant entities

- **Land Transport Authority (LTA)** — plans, develops, regulates and maintains land-transport systems. [[S1]](#s1)
- **Public Transport Council (PTC)** — regulates public-transport fares and reviews fare adjustments. [[S5]](#s5)
- **Rail operators** — provide rail services under the framework described by LTA. [[S2]](#s2)
- **Bus operators** — are licensed by LTA under the Bus Contracting Model. [[S3]](#s3)

## Singapore / Southeast Asia angle

This Briefing explains Singapore's local public-transport system and its own institutions. It does not compare Singapore's network, fares or operators with other cities. That comparison would need carefully matched, dated evidence.

## Discussion points, not positions

Useful questions for a conversation:

- Is the issue about getting to the network, the ride, a transfer, or the last leg?
- Is it about planning infrastructure, running a service, or regulating fares?
- What information is current and therefore needs a date: a route, fare, service change, project or disruption?
- Whose experience is being described, and what access needs shape it?

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **“Public transport means only MRT.”** Rail is important, but buses and access/last-leg connections are also part of the journey system. [[S1]](#s1) [[S3]](#s3) [[S4]](#s4)
- **“LTA and operators do the same job.”** LTA's official descriptions distinguish its planning, builder and regulatory roles from operators' service-provision roles. [[S1]](#s1) [[S2]](#s2) [[S3]](#s3)
- **“A feeder and a trunk service are interchangeable.”** LTA gives them different network functions. [[S3]](#s3)
- **“A current fare, route or delay belongs in an evergreen explanation.”** It needs a date and a current official source.
