## One-sentence explanation

Generative AI can produce new text, images, audio or code from an instruction or other input, but people still need to decide whether the output is accurate, appropriate and safe to use. [[S1]](#s1) [[S2]](#s2)

## 30-second overview

“Generative” means making new material rather than simply retrieving a stored answer. A person or organisation gives a system an input; the system produces an output; and people remain responsible for deciding what the output means and whether it can be used. [[S1]](#s1) [[S2]](#s2)

An output can sound convincing and still be incomplete, wrong, biased, unsuitable for its context, or unsupported by evidence. A useful habit is to ask: **what was this checked against?** [[S2]](#s2)

## The five-minute explanation

### Generating is not the same as knowing

Generative AI is useful because it can help people create or transform material. That does not make its output an authority. A generated answer is material to examine; a source is evidence that a reader can inspect. The two should not be confused. [[S1]](#s1)

This matters especially when an answer contains a factual claim, a quotation, a legal or medical statement, or a reference. The reader still needs a trustworthy underlying source, rather than treating the model's confidence as proof.

### Why governance is part of the picture

Singapore's AI-governance approach describes issues such as internal governance and accountability, human involvement, operations management, and communication with stakeholders. [[S2]](#s2) These are guidance principles, not a guarantee that every AI use is safe, fair or lawful.

The practical point is simple: responsible use is not only about whether a tool can produce an output. It is also about who is accountable, how the output is checked, what context it will be used in, and how people affected by it are treated.

### A practical example: a generated meeting summary

Imagine a team uses a generative system to produce a draft meeting summary.

1. The meeting notes or recording are the **input**.
2. The system produces a suggested summary — the **output**.
3. A person checks names, decisions, actions and sensitive details against the original material.
4. The team decides whether the checked version is suitable to share.

The example shows why a useful draft and a reliable record are different things. It does not say that any particular tool is suitable for meeting material.

## Why people care

Generative AI is showing up in work, study, creativity, customer service and public discussion. It can make some tasks faster, but it also raises questions about accuracy, privacy, fairness, intellectual property, accountability and misinformation.

The conversation becomes clearer when people name the actual use: Is the system helping create a first draft? Making a recommendation? Handling personal information? Supporting a decision that affects someone? Each calls for different checks.

## Singapore / Southeast Asia angle

Singapore's public discussion includes both innovation and trust. IMDA and PDPC materials provide a local governance lens, but guidance should not be mistaken for a blanket approval of a particular tool or use case. [[S1]](#s1) [[S2]](#s2)

This Briefing does not compare models, providers or countries. Those comparisons are fast-moving and need specific, dated evidence.

## Discussion points, not positions

Useful questions for a conversation:

- What is the system being asked to generate, and who will use it?
- What underlying source or record can check the important claims?
- Who is accountable if the output is wrong or harms someone?
- Is the issue about a useful draft, an automated decision, or something else entirely?

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **“A fluent answer is proof.”** Generated material still needs checking against appropriate evidence. [[S1]](#s1) [[S2]](#s2)
- **“Generative AI is one single capability.”** Different tools and uses have different limits and risks.
- **“Governance guidance guarantees a result.”** Guidance frames responsible practices; it does not guarantee safety, fairness or legal compliance in every case. [[S2]](#s2)
- **“A current product claim belongs in an evergreen explanation.”** Capability claims, prices, policy changes and provider announcements need dates and current sources.
