## One-sentence explanation

Singapore is organised around three branches of the state: the Legislature
makes laws, the Executive formulates policy and runs the state, and the
Judiciary interprets and applies the law. [\[S1\]](#s1)

## 30-second overview

The Constitution is Singapore's supreme law. It provides the basic framework
for the Executive, Legislature, and Judiciary. [\[S1\]](#s1)

The Legislature is Parliament, which makes laws. The Executive includes Cabinet
and public-service agencies under its charge; Cabinet gives the Government its
general direction and is accountable to Parliament. The Judiciary is the court
system, which interprets and enforces the law impartially. [\[S1\]](#s1) [\[S6\]](#s6)

The President is Head of State and is not a member of any of the three
branches. The Prime Minister is the effective head of the Executive and chairs
Cabinet. [\[S1\]](#s1)

## The five-minute explanation

### Different jobs in one constitutional system

The Legislature makes laws. The Executive formulates policy and administers the
state. The Judiciary interprets and enforces laws when deciding cases.
[\[S1\]](#s1)

They are connected, but they are not the same thing. Parliament does not run
every public service; courts do not set government policy; and the Executive
does not decide court cases. The Singapore Courts describes this separation of
powers as a structure designed to provide checks and balances. [\[S1\]](#s1)

### How a law becomes a law

A **Bill** is a draft law. Bills normally go through a First Reading, Second
Reading, Committee stage, and Third Reading in Parliament. The stages give
Parliament a way to introduce, debate, and examine a Bill. [\[S3\]](#s3)

After Parliament passes a Bill, it is sent to the President for assent. With
assent, it becomes an Act of Parliament. [\[S3\]](#s3)

The useful reader question is: “Is this a policy proposal, a Bill, or a law
already in force?” The answer helps identify which part of the system is under
discussion.

### Ministries, departments, and statutory boards

For a first reading, it is enough to know that the Executive includes
public-service agencies under Cabinet's charge. [\[S1\]](#s1) A ministry can
have statutory boards and government departments under its purview; the
Ministry of Trade and Industry is one official example. [\[S4\]](#s4)

These labels are not interchangeable. Check the official service page or
Singapore Government Directory for the body responsible for a particular
service rather than assuming it from a policy name. [\[S5\]](#s5)

## President, Prime Minister, and Cabinet

| Role | Main purpose in this model | Do not confuse it with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **President** | The President is Head of State. The President has constitutional powers, including a role at the final stage of legislation; in most areas, the President acts on Cabinet's advice, subject to constitutional exceptions. [\[S1\]](#s1) [\[S6\]](#s6) | The President is not a member of the Legislature, Executive, or Judiciary. [\[S1\]](#s1) |
| **Prime Minister** | The Prime Minister is the effective head of the Executive and chairs Cabinet. [\[S1\]](#s1) | The Prime Minister is not Head of State. [\[S1\]](#s1) |
| **Cabinet** | Cabinet is responsible for the Government's general direction and is accountable to Parliament. It is the Executive's central decision-making body. [\[S6\]](#s6) | Cabinet is not Parliament, and it is not the whole public service. [\[S1\]](#s1) |

**Who runs Singapore day to day?** The Prime Minister leads Cabinet, and
Cabinet gives the Government its general direction. The Executive includes
Cabinet and public-service agencies under its charge. [\[S1\]](#s1) [\[S6\]](#s6)

## A practical example: from policy idea to implementation

Imagine the Government is considering a change that needs a new law.

1. The relevant part of the Executive develops and considers the policy. The
   Executive formulates policy and administers the state. [\[S1\]](#s1)
2. If a law is needed, a Bill may be introduced in Parliament. Bills are
   usually introduced by a Minister on behalf of the Government, though any MP
   may introduce a Private Member's Bill. [\[S2\]](#s2)
3. Parliament considers the Bill through its readings and other applicable
   stages. [\[S3\]](#s3)
4. After Parliament passes it, the Bill goes to the President for assent. With
   assent, it becomes an Act. [\[S3\]](#s3)
5. The Executive administers the policy and law through the relevant
   public-service agencies. [\[S1\]](#s1)
6. If a legal case arises, the Judiciary decides it according to the law.
   [\[S1\]](#s1)

This is an orientation example, not a claim that every policy change needs a
new Act or that every law is challenged in court.

## Why people care

When a conversation mentions Parliament, Cabinet, a ministry, a statutory
board, the President, or a court, the three-branches map gives a starting
question: **which job in the system is being discussed?**

That question can prevent common mix-ups. A debate about a Bill is not the
same as a court case. A service run by a public agency is not the same as a
parliamentary vote. A Head of State is not automatically the day-to-day head
of the Executive. [\[S1\]](#s1)

## Singapore / Southeast Asia angle

This Briefing is specifically about Singapore's constitutional structure. It
does not compare Singapore's system with another country's system. Keeping
comparison out of this baseline lets a reader understand the local map first.

## Discussion points, not positions

This Briefing explains institutional roles. It does not take a side on a
policy, party, candidate, or court case.

Useful conversation questions:

- Is the issue about making a law, implementing a policy, or applying a law?
- Which public institution has the formal job being discussed?
- Is a statement a durable role, or a current development that needs a date and
  a source?
- Does the source distinguish a Bill, an Act, a policy, and a court decision?

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **“The President runs the Government day to day.”** The President is Head of
  State; the Prime Minister is the effective head of the Executive and chairs
  Cabinet. [\[S1\]](#s1)
- **“Parliament and Cabinet are the same.”** Parliament is the Legislature;
  Cabinet is part of the Executive. [\[S1\]](#s1)
- **“A Bill is already a law.”** A Bill is a draft law; it becomes an Act only
  after the required process, including Presidential assent. [\[S2\]](#s2)
- **“Courts make government policy.”** Courts decide cases according to the
  law; the Executive formulates policy. [\[S1\]](#s1)
- **“A current political claim belongs in this evergreen map.”** Current
  developments need their own dated source and, if published, a Current Update.
