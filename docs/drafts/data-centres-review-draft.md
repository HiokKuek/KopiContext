## One-sentence explanation

A data centre is physical infrastructure that helps run digital services by bringing computing, storage and networking together; it also needs electricity, cooling and careful resource planning. [[S1]](#s1)

## 30-second overview

When someone uses an app, watches a stream, saves a file online or uses a business system, the service depends on physical computing infrastructure somewhere. A data centre is one place that can house and operate that infrastructure. [[S1]](#s1)

That makes data centres a digital-connectivity question and a resource question at the same time. Singapore’s Green Data Centre Roadmap frames continued growth alongside energy efficiency and green-energy considerations. It does not say that digital services have no physical or environmental trade-offs. [[S1]](#s1)

## The five-minute explanation

### “Cloud” still has a physical side

“Cloud” is a convenient way to describe services delivered over networks. It should not be taken to mean that the computing behind those services has disappeared. Data centres provide a physical setting for computing, storage, networking and the services that depend on them. [[S1]](#s1)

The exact design differs from one facility or service to another. This Briefing therefore does not treat a data centre as a single kind of building or claim that every online service uses the same arrangement.

### Why resources are part of the conversation

The equipment inside a facility needs power, and operations involve cooling and other resource considerations. IMDA’s roadmap places data-centre development in a policy context that considers sustainable continued growth, energy efficiency and green-energy considerations. [[S1]](#s1)

That is a balancing problem, not a shortcut to a single answer. Digital capability, reliable connectivity, energy use, land and other resource considerations can all matter. Calling a facility or policy “green” should not be treated as proof that every trade-off has been resolved.

### Efficiency has more than one layer

Energy efficiency can concern how a facility is run, and also the IT equipment and workloads inside it. [[S1]](#s1) A reader should ask what is actually being measured before comparing claims about efficiency.

For example, a claim about the building’s systems is not automatically a claim about every application running inside it. Specific efficiency measures, benchmarks and performance figures need a dated method, scope and source before they can be used in a Current Update.

### A practical example: opening a shared file

Imagine a team opens a file in an online workspace.

1. The service receives and sends information over a network.
2. Computing, storage and networking equipment process or retrieve the file.
3. That equipment operates in physical infrastructure that needs reliable power and suitable operating conditions.
4. Decisions about digital capacity therefore connect to planning for efficiency, resilience and resources.

The example explains the relationship; it does not identify where any particular file is stored or make a claim about a named provider.

## Why people care

Data centres sit behind services used for work, communication, entertainment, public services and business operations. They also appear in discussions about AI, economic activity, national connectivity, electricity demand, land and sustainability.

When a headline says that more “cloud” or data-centre capacity is needed, it is useful to ask what service, resource, policy choice or measurement the claim is actually about.

## Singapore / Southeast Asia angle

Singapore’s Green Data Centre Roadmap is a local policy lens for understanding why digital infrastructure, sustainability and resource planning are discussed together. [[S1]](#s1) It is not a standing approval of a particular project, allocation or technology.

This baseline avoids changing capacity figures, company announcements and comparisons with other places. Those need separately sourced, dated treatment.

## Debates and trade-offs

The central trade-off is not simply “digital growth versus the environment.” A useful conversation asks how reliable, connected digital capability can be planned alongside electricity use, efficiency, land and other resource constraints. Different proposals may give different weight to those aims. This Briefing does not prescribe a preferred outcome.

## Discussion points, not positions

- Which part of the system is under discussion: the service, the equipment or the facility?
- What resource or system boundary is a performance claim measuring?
- Is a statement about a policy direction, a target, a project proposal or an operating result?
- What current source supports a capacity or efficiency figure?

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **“Cloud services have no physical infrastructure.”** Online services still depend on computing, storage, networking and facilities. [[S1]](#s1)
- **“A data centre is only an IT issue.”** Its operation also connects to energy, cooling, land and resource planning. [[S1]](#s1)
- **“Green means there are no trade-offs.”** Policy framing around sustainability does not remove the need to assess resource choices. [[S1]](#s1)
- **“One efficiency figure settles the question.”** A number needs its dated methodology and system boundary.
